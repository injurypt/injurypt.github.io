🦵 ACL Rehabilitation Protocol

Rehabilitation Phases
1

Weeks 1–2
Control swelling, restore ROM, activate VMO

02Heel Slides3×15, Slow & controlledAim 90°+ flexion by end of week 2.
03Straight Leg Raises3×15, 4s eccentricBrace core, no quad lag allowed.
04Ankle Pumps5×20, ContinuousDVT prevention — every 2 hours.
05Patellar Mobilizations2×2min, All directionsPrevent infrapatellar fibrosis.
06RICE Protocol4–6×/day, 20 min eachIce + compression post-exercise.

▲ Precautions & Contraindications
▲Avoid open kinetic chain knee extension 0–60° until Week 12+ (graft stress zone)
▲No contact sports before 9 months — ligamentization (graft remodeling) is not complete
▲Ice + elevation 20 min after every session for the first 8 weeks
▲Use LSI (Limb Symmetry Index) — never time alone — for all return-to-sport decisions
● Key Outcome Measures
LSILimb Symmetry Index — quad strength via isokinetic dynamometer
ACL-RSIReturn to Sport after Injury scale — psychological readiness (0–100)
IKDCInternational Knee Documentation Committee — subjective knee function
Hop TestsSingle, triple, 6m timed, crossover — functional symmetry 90%+
⚙ Manual Therapy
Patellar MobilizationsSuperior/inferior and medial/lateral glides to maintain patellar mobility and prevent infrapatellar contracture post-ACLR.
Tibiofemoral Joint GlidesPosterior Grade III-IV glides to restore full knee extension. Anterior glides for flexion deficits.
ITB & Quad Soft Tissue ReleaseMyofascial release targeting lateral retinaculum, ITB, and quad to address post-surgical tightness.
Scar Tissue MobilizationCross-friction massage and cross-massage over incision sites to prevent adhesions and improve tissue mobility.
⚡ Modalities
Cryotherapy with CompressionIce + intermittent pneumatic compression 20 min post-session to manage effusion and pain.
NMES for QuadricepsNeuromuscular electrical stimulation at 50Hz, 10s on/50s off during quad sets to combat arthrogenic muscle inhibition.
TENSHigh-frequency TENS over peripatellar region for pain modulation during early weight-bearing.
Blood Flow Restriction (BFR)Low-load BFR at 20-30% 1RM during open-chain knee extensions to enhance hypertrophy in early phases.
🏃 Functional Training
Single-Leg Squat ProgressionMirror-box feedback ensuring dynamic knee valgus control. Progress from partial to full depth.
Agility Ladder DrillsForward/backward shuffles, lateral shuffles, and carioca for neuromuscular control and deceleration.
Plyometric ProgressionDouble-leg hops to single-leg hops, depth jumps, box jumps focusing on soft landings and limb symmetry.
Sport-Specific Cutting Drills45 and 90 degree cutting at progressive speeds. Video analysis for knee-over-toe alignment.
📋 Patient Education
Graft Healing TimelineBiological graft incorporation takes 6-12 months. Avoid high-risk pivoting for minimum 9 months post-op.
Swelling ManagementElevation, compression, and ice for 15-20 min 4-6x/day. Expect intermittent effusion for 6-12 months.
Brace ProtocolROM brace per surgeon specifications. Lock at 0 degrees for weight-bearing in phase 1. Gradually unlock per protocol.
Return-to-Sport StatisticsOnly 55% of athletes return to pre-injury level. Re-injury risk 6x higher if returning before 9 months.
🏠 Home Exercise Program (HEP)
Frequency & Duration5-7x/week during phases 1-2. Each session 45-60 min. Reduce to 4-5x/week in phases 3-4 with higher intensity.
Phase 1-2 FocusQuad sets, SLR, heel slides, prone hangs, standing calf raises, stationary bike. 3x15-20 reps per exercise.
Phase 3-4 FocusLeg press, hamstring curls, single-leg press, Bulgarian split squats, leg extensions (open chain from 90 degrees).
Compliance TargetMinimum 80% HEP adherence required. Log all exercises with pain scores. Document ROM gains weekly.
🚫 Red Flags - Stop Treatment & Refer
Joint EffusionNew or increasing effusion after initial resolution may indicate infection, graft complication, or reaction. Refer urgently.
Extension Loss >5 degreesPersistent or new extension deficit suggests cyclops lesion, arthrofibrosis, or graft impingement. Orthopedic review.
Giving-Way EpisodesAny subjective or objective instability episode post-ACLR indicates potential graft failure. Urgent surgical evaluation.
Signs of DVTCalf pain, swelling, warmth, Homans sign. Lower limb DVT risk post-surgery. Urgent medical review and Doppler.
✅ Return to Activity Criteria
LSI Quadriceps & HamstringsIsokinetic strength symmetry index >=90% at 60 degrees/sec and 180 degrees/sec bilaterally.
Hop Test BatterySingle hop, triple hop, crossover hop, 6m timed hop - all >=90% LSI with good quality movement.
Psychological ReadinessACL-RSI scale >=56/100. Address kinesiophobia with graded exposure and sport visualization.
Time-Based CriterionMinimum 9 months post-ACLR before return to contact sports. 12 months preferred for high-risk pivoting sports.

⚙ Manual Therapy
Posterior Capsule StretchSleeper stretch and cross-body adduction to address glenohumeral internal rotation deficit (GIRD).
Glenohumeral Joint MobilizationsGrade III-IV posterior and inferior glides to restore capsular mobility and arthrokinematics.
AC & SC Joint MobilizationsGrade I-II oscillations for pain relief at acromioclavicular and sternoclavicular joints.
Scapular RepositioningManual scapular retraction and downward rotation mobilization with patient active engagement.
⚡ Modalities
Therapeutic UltrasoundContinuous 1MHz at 1.5 W/cm2 for deep rotator cuff tissue. Pulsed for acute inflammatory phase.
TENS for Pain ModulationHigh-frequency TENS over deltoid and periscapular region pre and post exercise.
Low-Level Laser TherapyClass II laser at 5mW over supraspinatus insertion for tendon healing promotion.
NMES for Rotator CuffElectrical stimulation during side-lying external rotation to re-educate infraspinatus and teres minor.
🏃 Functional Training
Closed Kinetic Chain (CKC)Wall push-ups, bear crawls, quadruped perturbations for scapulohumeral rhythm.
Proprioceptive NeuromuscularRhythmic stabilization at 0, 45, 90 degrees abduction. Isometric co-contraction drills.
Scapular Stabilization SeriesProne Y-T-W-L raises, serratus punches, dynamic planks for scapular dyskinesis.
Overhead Athlete ProgressionThrowing program progression: 50% distance to full distance to velocity to competitive.
📋 Patient Education
RC Pathology EducationRotator cuff tendinopathy involves supraspinatus/infraspinatus. Impingement = mechanical compression under acromion.
Postural AwarenessAvoid sustained protraction and forward head posture. Ergonomic workspace modification.
Sleep PositioningSide-lying with pillow between arm to prevent adduction/internal rotation. Avoid sleeping on affected side.
Activity ModificationAvoid repetitive overhead activities in early phases. Limit abduction >90 degrees.
🏠 Home Exercise Program (HEP)
Frequency5-6x/week for phases 1-2. Daily pendulum and scapular exercises. 4-5x/week for phases 3-4.
Phase 1-2 ExercisesPendulums 2 min, isometric IR/ER 5x10s holds, scapular squeezes 3x15, wall slides 3x12.
Phase 3-4 ExercisesSide-lying ER 3x15, prone Y-T-W 3x10, resisted band exercises, push-up plus 3x15.
Compliance ExpectationsTrack daily with exercise log. Pain should not exceed 3/10 during exercises. ROM measured weekly.
🚫 Red Flags - Stop Treatment & Refer
Complete RC Tear SignsSulcus sign, inability to initiate abduction, positive drop arm test. Urgent orthopedic referral for possible surgical repair.
Anterior Instability EpisodesRecurrent subluxation or dislocation events. Indicates labral pathology or capsular laxity requiring surgical consult.
Cervical Radiculopathy SignsNumbness, tingling, weakness in arm/hand. Differentiate from shoulder pathology. Refer for imaging.
Tumor/Infection SignsUnexplained weight loss, night pain unrelieved by position change, fever. Urgent medical workup.
✅ Return to Activity Criteria
Pain-Free Active ROMFull active forward flexion >=170 degrees, abduction >=170 degrees, ER at side >=70 degrees without pain.
Strength TestingManual muscle testing 5/5 all RC muscle groups. ER/IR strength ratio >=66% (ER:IR).
Functional TestingClosed Kinetic Chain Upper Extremity Stability Test (CKCUEST) >=18 touches in 15 seconds.
Sport-Specific CriteriaOverhead athletes: pain-free throwing at full velocity for 4+ weeks. Y-Balance test symmetry.

● Key Outcome Measures
NRSNumeric Rating Scale 0–10 — pain intensity before/after each session
ODIOswestry Disability Index — target <20% = minimal disability
PSFSPatient-Specific Functional Scale — 3 personal goals rated 0–10
CentralizationPain moves from periphery to center — key McKenzie outcome predictor
⚙ Manual Therapy
McKenzie Directional PreferenceCentralization-proving repeated extension or flexion movements. Establish direction of preference first.
Lumbar Segmental MobilizationGrade III-IV PA mobilizations to hypomobile segments adjacent to the painful level.
Neural MobilizationSlump neural glides and straight leg raise neurodynamics to address neural mechanosensitivity.
Muscle Energy TechniquesPositional lumbar rotation MET for segmental restriction. Psoas and QL lengthening.
⚡ Modalities
Heat TherapySuperficial moist heat 15-20 min pre-exercise to reduce paraspinal muscle guarding.
Lumbar TractionMechanical intermittent traction at 50-60% body weight for centralization and disc decompression.
TENS for Radicular PainHigh-frequency TENS over affected dermatome for neuropathic pain modulation.
Interferential Current (IFC)Deep-acting IFC targeting paraspinal musculature for muscle spasm reduction.
🏃 Functional Training
McKenzie Loading StrategyProgressive extension-based exercises following centralization direction. Prone press-ups 3x10 every 2 hours.
Core Stability ProgressionTransversus abdominis activation to dead bugs to bird dogs to planks to side planks.
Hip Hinge TrainingRomanian deadlift pattern with dowel to teach safe spinal mechanics. Avoid flexion under load initially.
Walking ProgramProgressive walking 15-30 min 2-3x/day. Incline treadmill walking for symptomatic relief.
📋 Patient Education
Disc Pathology EducationHerniation is a protrusion of nucleus pulposus through annulus fibrosus. 90% improve with conservative management.
Directional PreferenceAvoid combined flexion-rotation movements. Follow McKenzie directional preference throughout the day.
Sitting PostureLumbar support, hip angle >90 degrees. Take microbreaks every 30 min standing and extending. Avoid low chairs.
Red Flag AwarenessProgressive neurological deficit, bowel/bladder changes = cauda equina syndrome. Immediate surgical referral.
🏠 Home Exercise Program (HEP)
FrequencyExtension exercises every 2 hours when awake. Core stability 3-5x/week. Walking daily.
Phase 1 ExercisesProne press-ups 3x10, McKenzie extension in lying 3x10, walking 15-20 min 2-3x/day, diaphragmatic breathing.
Phase 2+ ExercisesDead bugs 3x12, bird dogs 3x10/side, side planks 3x20s, glute bridges 3x15, hip hinges with dowel.
Pain MonitoringCentralization (pain moving proximally) = good sign. Peripheralization (pain moving distally) = stop and reassess.
🚫 Red Flags - Stop Treatment & Refer
Cauda Equina SyndromeSaddle anesthesia, bilateral leg weakness, urinary retention/incontinence. EMERGENCY surgical decompression.
Progressive Neurological DeficitWorsening motor weakness (foot drop), progressive numbness in L4/L5/S1 dermatome. Urgent MRI and surgical consult.
Unrelenting Night PainPain that awakens from sleep and is not relieved by position change. Rule out tumor, infection, or fracture.
Constitutional SymptomsFever, chills, unexplained weight loss, IV drug use history. Rule out discitis, osteomyelitis, epidural abscess.
✅ Return to Activity Criteria
Pain-Free Functional CapacityNRS <=2/10 during all ADLs. Ability to sit 45 min, stand 30 min, walk 30 min without symptom aggravation.
Core EnduranceMcGill trunk extensor endurance >=120s, flexor endurance >=120s, side plank >=90s bilaterally.
Lumbar ROMFunctional flexion, extension, lateral flexion and rotation within 80% of normative values without neural symptoms.
Work HardeningGraduated return-to-work with job-specific physical demands testing. Full duty lifting capacity demonstrated.

▾
1. Attention Redirection Drills
10 min x 2-3x/day
Practice shifting attention away from symptom body part to an unaffected area. For functional tremor: focus attention on a stable limb. For functional weakness: use mirror therapy and visual feedback to redirect motor planning. Based on attention retraining principles from Physiotherapy for Functional Neurological Disorders (Rogers & Edwards).
2. Entrainment Therapy (Tremor)
15 min x 3x/week
Rhythmic movement of the affected limb to a metronome or external cue at a frequency above the tremor rate. Progressively increase speed and complexity. Goal is to demonstrate voluntary control over involuntary-appearing movements. Cochrane-reviewed evidence for FND tremor (Nielsen et al.).
3. Motor Imagery & Mental Practice
15 min x daily
Guided imagery of functional movements (walking, reaching, grasping). Use first-person perspective imagery progressing to third-person. Evidence from motor learning literature supports cortical reorganization through mental rehearsal (Jackson et al., 2001).
4. Graded Sensory Retraining
10 min x 2x/day
Systematic desensitisation using tactile, proprioceptive, and visual stimuli to the affected area. Progress from light touch to texture discrimination. Addresses cortical sensory processing abnormalities documented in functional sensory loss.
5. Functional Movement Activation
20 min x 3x/week
Voluntary activation of paralysed limbs using distraction techniques, mirror therapy, and bilateral coordination tasks. Record demonstrations of capability. Progress from single-plane to multi-planar functional patterns. Use feedback from video review to reinforce capability awareness.
6. Psychoeducation � Stress-Symptom Model
20 min x weekly
Teach the autonomic nervous system model of FND symptoms. Use biofeedback (HRV, GSR) to demonstrate how emotional states trigger or amplify symptoms. Develop personalised symptom diaries linking triggers to episodes.

▾

2. Dual-Task Training
15 min x 3x/week
Combine motor tasks with cognitive challenges (counting backwards, verbal fluency). Progress from simple dual-task (walking + talking) to complex (walking + carrying + navigating). Addresses attentional bias toward symptoms during functional activity.

Collaboratively develop a graded exposure ladder for activities avoided due to symptoms or fear. Use SUDs (Subjective Units of Distress) to track anxiety. Example for functional gait: standing in kitchen, walking to letterbox, walking in shopping centre, walking on uneven ground.
4. Contextual Interference Training
20 min x 2x/week
Practice functional movements in varied environments (clinic, home, outdoors) with random practice scheduling. Enhances motor learning transfer and reduces symptom context-dependency. Evidence from motor learning literature (Shea & Morgan, 1979) applied to FND retraining.
5. Group Functional Circuit
45 min x 2x/week
Supervised group exercise circuit with FND-specific stations: balance challenges, functional reaching, gait tasks, and relaxation. Provides normalisation, reduces isolation, and enables observational learning. Peer support enhances self-efficacy.
6. Cognitive Behavioural Strategies Integration
20 min x weekly
Incorporate CBT-based strategies for symptom management: identifying automatic thoughts about symptoms, cognitive restructuring, behavioural experiments. Coordinate with psychology team. Evidence-based for FND (Goldstein et al., 2010; Carson et al., 2016).

3. Passive Range of Motion
20 min x daily
Comprehensive passive ROM for all major joints: shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, and ankle. Prevent contractures and maintain comfort. Gentle, slow movements with attention to patient comfort responses. Use lotion for comfort during sessions.
4. Positioning & Pressure Care
Repositioning 2-4 hourly
Regular repositioning schedule for bedbound patients. Pressure-relieving mattresses and cushions. Heel and elbow protection. Positioning for respiratory function (30 deg elevation) and swallowing safety. Skin inspection at every position change.
5. Multi-Sensory Stimulation
20-30 min x daily
Snoezelen room or bedside sensory activities: tactile stimulation (textures, objects), olfactory (lavender, citrus), auditory (familiar music), and visual (light displays). Provides comfort and engagement when cognitive function is severely reduced.
6. Swallowing Management
Ongoing assessment
Positioning for safe swallowing (upright 90 deg, chin tuck). Texture modification as per SLT recommendations. Monitor for aspiration signs. Oral hygiene maintenance to reduce aspiration pneumonia risk. Fluid intake monitoring.
